Philosophy
Not a list of technologies. A way of approaching problems that hasn't changed across seven years and several very different domains.
01
The workaround is part of the design
Every system has places where the documented solution doesn't apply. I've learned to stop treating those as failures and start treating them as information. The workaround that unblocks progress usually reveals more about the actual problem than the original plan did. Some of the things I'm most proud of are the small moments where I figured out what nobody had written down yet.
02
Complexity doesn't disappear — it gets redistributed
When I build something other engineers or users depend on, my job is to absorb the hard parts so they don't encounter them. A good abstraction isn't one that hides things — it's one that hides the right things. If the person calling your API needs to understand how it works internally to use it safely, the abstraction is incomplete.
03
You don't understand a system until something breaks
On-call rotations changed how I build. Now when I'm writing code I'm also asking: what will I need to see at 2am, with no context and an incident already open? Observability isn't something you add later. It's how you prove you understood the system in the first place.
04
Curiosity compounds, familiarity atrophies
The most dangerous thing I can tell myself is "I know how this works." I try to stay in the habit of questioning the assumptions that feel settled. It's part of why I'm expanding into AI and quantum computing alongside my blockchain work — the curiosity that got me into this field is the same one that keeps me learning across domains.
05
Ship it and stay. The learning is in the maintenance
I've mostly worked 0→1, but I've always stayed past launch. That's where the real education is — in the incidents, the second versions, the things you didn't anticipate. A system you shipped and then handed off is a system you only half understood.
None of this is about a language or a stack. It's about staying curious enough to keep questioning the shape of the problem, long after most people have settled on an answer.
